# Strength & Power Training

Kali-specific strength needs: **grip endurance**, **forearm conditioning**, **rotational core power**, and **shoulder stability**.

---

## Grip & Forearm

### Rice Bucket Training

Classic martial arts forearm conditioner. Fill a bucket with dry rice and plunge your hands in repeatedly.

- Open-close grip: 3 × 30 seconds
- Finger extension against resistance: 3 × 30 seconds
- Wrist rotation in the rice: 3 × 30 seconds

**Why:** Builds grip endurance specific to stick work — not just raw squeeze strength.

---

### Stick Wrist Roller

Tie a weight (0.5–2 kg) to a short dowel with a cord. Roll the weight up by rotating the wrists alternately.

- 3 sets wrist-up (palms down)
- 3 sets wrist-down (palms up)
- Add weight as the exercise becomes easy

---

### Stick Rotations

Hold a stick at arm's length, parallel to the floor. Rotate the wrist to point the tip in circles.

- 20 inward, 20 outward — per hand
- Progress to a weighted stick

---

## Rotational Power (Core)

Kali strikes get their power from hip and core rotation, not arm strength. Train this directly.

### Medicine Ball Rotational Throw

- Stand sideways to a wall (or with a partner)
- Hold a 4–6 kg medicine ball at waist height
- Rotate hips and throw the ball against the wall explosively
- Catch the rebound and repeat
- 3 × 10 per side

### Pallof Press

Anti-rotation core exercise — builds the stability to anchor your rotation.

- Attach a resistance band to a fixed point at chest height
- Stand sideways, hold the band at your chest
- Press it straight out, hold 2 seconds, return
- 3 × 12 per side

---

## Shoulder Stability

### Band Pull-Aparts

- Hold a resistance band at chest height, arms extended
- Pull the band apart until it touches your chest
- Slow and controlled — 3 × 20

### Face Pulls

- Resistance band or cable at face height
- Pull toward your face, elbows high and wide
- 3 × 15 — important for shoulder health with high-volume striking

### Single-Arm Bottoms-Up Kettlebell Press

Challenges shoulder stability in an unstable grip — mimics the reactive grip of stick work.

## Weekly Schedule Suggestion

| Day | Focus |
|---|---|
| Monday | Technique (primary), grip work after |
| Tuesday | Rest or light conditioning |
| Wednesday | Technique + rotational power |
| Thursday | Rest |
| Friday | Technique + shoulder stability |
| Saturday | Sparring / partner drills |
| Sunday | Full rest or recovery |

---

!!! tip "Conditioning should not exhaust you for technique sessions"
    If your conditioning work is leaving your forearms or shoulders too sore to train Kali properly — you've done too much. Conditioning serves the art, not the other way around.

# Lineage & History

Understanding where Kali comes from deepens your practice and respect for the art.

---

## Origins

Kali / Arnis / Eskrima is the indigenous martial art of the Philippine archipelago. Evidence of blade-based fighting arts in the Philippines predates Spanish colonisation (1565). Spanish soldiers noted that Filipino warriors were already skilled fighters when they arrived — their blade arts were already mature.

The three names — **Kali**, **Arnis**, **Eskrima** — refer to the same family of arts but carry regional and lineage-specific connotations:

- **Kali**: Older, associated with Pekiti Tirsia and some Visayan systems
- **Arnis**: Associated with Modern Arnis, Remy Presas lineage; the official national sport name
- **Eskrima**: Common in Cebuano regions; popularised internationally alongside Kali

---

## Major Systems

| System | Key Figure(s) | Characteristics |
|---|---|---|
| **Modern Arnis** | Remy Presas | Emphasises tapi-tapi (checking), accessible to beginners |
| **Pekiti Tirsia Kali** | Leo Tortal Gaje Jr. | Blade-centric, triangular footwork, aggressive |
| **Inosanto Kali** | Dan Inosanto | Eclectic, synthesises many Filipino arts; widely taught in the West |
| **Sayoc Kali** | Sayoc family | Knife system, information-based training methodology |
| **Doce Pares** | Canete family | One of the oldest codified Cebuano systems |
| **Lameco Eskrima** | Edgar Sulite | Heavy on largo (long range); emphasis on footwork |

---

## Kali in MMA

Kali footwork, defanging, and blade concepts have been incorporated by fighters such as:

- **B.J. Penn** — known to have trained Kali
- **Anderson Silva** — studied Filipino arts among other systems

Dan Inosanto (student of Bruce Lee) was instrumental in spreading Kali to the West, embedding it into what became Jeet Kune Do concepts training.

# Glossary — Kali Terminology

Terms drawn from Filipino (Tagalog / Cebuano), Spanish-influenced Visayan, and common English transliterations used in training.

---

## A

**Abaniko** — "Fan." A fanning wrist-driven strike, circular in path.

**Aldabis** — A diagonal upward strike.

**Arnis** — The national martial art of the Philippines. One of three names for the same art.

---

## B

**Baston** — "Stick." The primary training weapon.

**Buno** — Filipino wrestling / grappling component.

---

## D

**Daga** — "Knife" or dagger. Also refers to knife fighting techniques.

**Defanging the snake** — Targeting the weapon hand / forearm rather than the body. Neutralise the weapon first.

**Depensa** — "Defence." Refers to defensive stance and defensive techniques.

**Doble Olisi** — Double stick. Both hands hold weapons.

---

## E

**Eskrima / Escrima** — Another name for the Filipino martial arts system. Derived from the Spanish *esgrima* (fencing).

---

## G

**Gunting** — "Scissors." A defanging technique where you intercept and cut/strike the weapon arm.

---

## H

**Hakbang** — "Step." Refers to footwork patterns.

**Hakbang Palit** — "Replacement step." Switching lead foot position.

---

## K

**Kali** — The oldest name for the Filipino martial art. Used especially in systems with older lineage. Sometimes considered an acronym: *KA* (kamay/hands) + *LI* (likha/creativity).

**Kamay** — "Hand." Also refers to the alive hand.

---

## L

**Largo Mano** — "Long range." Attacking from the maximum range of the weapon (tip zone). Footwork-intensive.

**Live hand / Alive hand** — The non-weapon hand; always active in checking, trapping, or guarding.

---

## O

**Olisi** — "Stick." Another term for baston.

---

## P

**Palis-Palis** — A passing deflection that redirects an incoming strike without meeting it directly.

**Palyo** — A thrust or stab.

**Palusot** — "To slip through." Techniques that pass inside or outside a weapon.

**Payong** — "Umbrella." The umbrella block — stick held overhead horizontally.

**Pekiti** — "To cut into small pieces." As in Pekiti Tirsia Kali, a major system.

**Punyo** — The butt end of a stick. Used as a short-range striking surface.

---

## R

**Redondo** — "Round / circular." Circular wrist-driven striking motion.

---

## S

**Sinawali** — "To weave." Double-stick flowing patterns. Also used as a two-person drill format.

**Sombrada** — "Shadow / umbrella." A flowing partner drill — strike, block, counter.

---

## T

**Triángulo** — "Triangle." The triangular footwork stepping pattern.

---

## W

**Witik** — A flicking wrist-snap strike, similar to abaniko but typically shorter.

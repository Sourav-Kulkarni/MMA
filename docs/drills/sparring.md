# Sparring

Sparring is where everything gets tested. The goal is **not winning** — it is **learning under pressure**.

---

## Protective Gear (Required)

!!! danger "Do not skip gear"
    Rattan at speed to an unprotected head can cause serious injury. Gear up, every time.

| Gear | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Full face mask / fencing helmet | Head, face, neck protection |
| Padded gloves (Escrima gloves) | Hand and knuckle protection |
| Forearm guards | Block incoming strikes on the forearm |
| Cup / groin guard | Low attacks happen in live sparring |
| Knee pads (optional but recommended) | Ground game / footwork falls |

---

## Sparring Protocols

=== "Slow Flow Sparring"
    **Goal:** Application, not competition. Both partners move at 30–40% speed.

    Rules:
    - Light contact only — touches score, power doesn't
    - No head shots with full power — ever
    - If a hit lands cleanly, acknowledge it ("yes") and reset or continue
    - Stop and debrief interesting exchanges

    **Best for:** Beginners and as a warm-up for all levels.

=== "Technical Sparring"
    **Goal:** Apply a specific technique or drill concept.

    Rules:
    - One person has an assigned technique (e.g. "must counter every attack with defang")
    - Other person attacks freely at moderate speed
    - Debrief after each round: did the technique work? Why/why not?

=== "Live Sparring"
    **Goal:** Realistic application with full protective gear.

    Rules:
    - Contact is allowed but controlled
    - No strikes to the spine or back of the head
    - Referee or partner present
    - Stop on a clear submission or "stop" signal

## Mindset for Sparring

!!! tip "Get hit, learn from it"
    Every time you get hit in sparring, something was wrong: your guard, your footwork, your timing, your angle. Treat each hit as a data point.

!!! tip "The goal is information, not ego"
    Sparring with a better practitioner is a gift. Do not avoid it because you lose — that's exactly where the fastest learning happens.

---

## Post-Sparring Debrief (Do This Every Time)

After each sparring session, write down:

1. What worked well?
2. What kept getting through my guard?
3. One specific thing to fix next session

Over months, this log becomes your most valuable training resource.

---

## Sparring Drill: One-Tool Sparring

Both partners are restricted to **one technique only**. Example:

- Both can only use Angle 1 and Angle 2
- Both can only thrust (Angle 5)
- One can only strike, the other can only block and defang

Constraints force deeper exploration of a limited tool set.

    12 Angles of Attack¶

    Imagine a clock face standing vertically in front of you, with 12 at the top and 6 at the bottom. Every strike in Kali travels along one of these clock lines. Learn to see these lines and you can read — and predict — any attack.

    Note

    Numbering varies between systems. This follows the clock-face framework used in class. When in doubt, defer to your instructor's numbering.

    The 12 Angles¶

    Angle 1 — Forehand Diagonal (1 to 7)¶

    Strike travels from roughly 1 o'clock down to 7 o'clock — a downward diagonal from your right shoulder toward the opponent's lower left.

    • Target: Left temple, left collarbone, left shoulder
    • Hand: Dominant (forehand)
    • Motion: The elbow leads upward to the wind-up position near 1 o'clock, then the forearm drops and the wrist snaps through toward 7 o'clock
    • Key point: Power comes from hip rotation and wrist snap at contact — not a shoulder swing

    Angle 2 — Backhand Diagonal (11 to 5)¶

    Strike travels from roughly 11 o'clock down to 5 o'clock — a downward diagonal from your left shoulder toward the opponent's lower right. Mirror of Angle 1.

    • Target: Right temple, right collarbone, right shoulder
    • Hand: Dominant (backhand)
    • Motion: Elbow draws back and up toward 11 o'clock, forearm drops through toward 5 o'clock with a wrist snap
    • Key point: The elbow must stay up — dropping it below shoulder level telegraphs the strike

    Angle 3 — Forehand Horizontal (9 to 3)¶

    Strike travels horizontally from 9 o'clock to 3 o'clock — a straight cross from your left to your right at mid-body height.

    • Target: Left ribs, left hip, left knee (depending on height)
    • Hand: Dominant (forehand)
    • Motion: A lateral sweeping motion; keep the tip level throughout — do not let it drop or rise mid-arc

    Angle 4 — Backhand Horizontal (3 to 9)¶

    Reverse of Angle 3. Strike travels from 3 o'clock to 9 o'clock.

    • Target: Right ribs, right hip
    • Hand: Dominant (backhand)
    • Motion: Mirror of Angle 3 — same flat horizontal plane, opposite direction

    Angle 5 — Thrust (12 to 6, straight in)¶

    Not a clock sweep — a linear thrust driving the tip straight forward toward the target.

    • Target: Throat, solar plexus, face
    • Motion: Extend from the elbow and shoulder. Think of punching the tip forward, not pushing
    • Key point: Retraction is half the technique. A thrust that stalls leaves you overextended and open

    Retract immediately

    After any thrust, pull back sharply along the same line. Lingering is dangerous.

    Angle 6 — Forehand Upward Diagonal (7 to 1)¶

    The reverse of Angle 1 — rising from lower left (7 o'clock) up toward upper right (1 o'clock).

    • Target: Groin, inner forearm, weapon hand (defanging)
    • Hand: Dominant (forehand, rising)
    • Motion: Strike rises from low right-to-left; the wrist snaps upward at contact

    Angle 7 — Backhand Upward Diagonal (5 to 11)¶

    The reverse of Angle 2 — rising from lower right (5 o'clock) up toward upper left (11 o'clock).

    • Target: Groin, inner forearm, weapon hand
    • Hand: Dominant (backhand, rising)

    Angle 8 — Forehand Low Horizontal (9 to 3, low)¶

    Same direction as Angle 3 but dropped to knee/shin height. The clock face is now read at the lower third.

    • Target: Left knee, left shin
    • Key point: Requires a slight body drop or squat — do not just tilt the stick downward while staying upright

    Angle 9 — Backhand Low Horizontal (3 to 9, low)¶

    Mirror of Angle 8 at low level.

    • Target: Right knee, right shin

    Angle 10 — Downward Thrust (12 to 6, downward)¶

    A stabbing motion driving straight downward — 12 o'clock to 6 o'clock — into a target below you.

    • Target: Top of the foot, downed opponent
    • Grip: Often switches to an icepick grip for this angle
    • Context: Close-range, grappling situations

    Angle 11 — Forehand Upper Diagonal (3 to 9, high)¶

    A high forehand strike traveling from 3 o'clock upward to 9 o'clock — rising from the right side toward the upper left.

    • Target: Left jaw, left neck, left ear
    • Variation: Often executed as an upward abaniko (fanning wrist strike)

    Angle 12 — Crown (straight down, 12 to 6)¶

    A direct overhead downward strike — 12 o'clock straight down to 6 o'clock — landing on the crown of the head.

    • Target: Top of the skull, back of the neck
    • Defence: Roof block or payong (umbrella) block

    Quick Reference¶

    Angle Clock Line Direction Primary Target
    1 1 → 7 Forehand diagonal down Left temple / collarbone
    2 11 → 5 Backhand diagonal down Right temple / collarbone
    3 9 → 3 Forehand horizontal Left ribs / hip
    4 3 → 9 Backhand horizontal Right ribs / hip
    5 Straight in Thrust Throat / solar plexus
    6 7 → 1 Forehand diagonal up Groin / weapon hand
    7 5 → 11 Backhand diagonal up Groin / weapon hand
    8 9 → 3 (low) Forehand horizontal low Left knee / shin
    9 3 → 9 (low) Backhand horizontal low Right knee / shin
    10 Straight down Downward thrust Top of foot
    11 3 → 9 (high) Forehand upward diagonal Left jaw / neck
    12 12 → 6 Overhead straight down Crown of head

    Drilling the Angles¶

    Strike each angle in sequence, 10 reps each. First pass: slow and deliberate, full body rotation. Second pass: full speed.

    Label zones on a wall pad with clock positions. Call the angle to yourself before striking. Progress: have a partner call angles at random — react, don't anticipate.

    Feeder throws each angle slowly. Receiver names the angle aloud before blocking. Builds the recognition-to-response chain before speed is introduced.

    Angles 1 and 2 first

    The forehand and backhand diagonals are the most common attacks you will face and throw. Get these two deeply ingrained before moving on.

    Train both hands

    Every angle, both hands. Your non-dominant side will lag — that is normal and will close with time.
